# Week 1: Getting Started with Agentic AI as a TPM

**Date:** January 2026  
**Focus:** Foundation building and first experiments

## What I Built This Week

Started with the most painful part of my TPM workflow: meeting preparation and follow-up. Built a simple Meeting Intelligence Agent using Gemini and prompt templates.

**Scope:** Pre-meeting context gathering and post-meeting summary generation for my weekly program syncs.

## The "Aha!" Moments

### 1. Prompt quality > Tool complexity
I started trying to build a complex automation pipeline. Then I realized a well-crafted prompt in Gemini got me 80% of the value in 20% of the time. 

**Lesson:** Start with prompts, automate only what's truly repetitive.

### 2. Context is everything
My first attempt: "Summarize these meeting notes." 
Result: Generic, useless summary.

My second attempt: Provided meeting title, attendees, program context, and last meeting summary.
Result: Actually useful brief that saved me 15 minutes of prep.

**Lesson:** AI needs the same context you'd give a human colleague. Don't skip the setup.

### 3. Templates scale your learning
Once I had a prompt that worked for one meeting type, I could adapt it to other meetings in minutes. Now I have templates for:
- Daily standups
- Weekly program syncs  
- Stakeholder reviews
- Sprint planning

**Lesson:** Build templates, not one-offs. Your future self will thank you.

## What Worked

✅ **Pre-meeting context gathering**: Cut prep time from 20 min to 5 min per meeting. The AI pulls relevant info from previous meetings, current sprint docs, and open issues.

✅ **Action item extraction**: More accurate than my manual notes. The AI catches items I missed while actively participating in discussions.

✅ **Consistent documentation**: Every meeting now has the same structured summary format. Makes it easy to find decisions and track patterns over time.

## What Didn't Work

❌ **Full automation too early**: Tried to automate everything from calendar trigger to email distribution. Spent 3 hours debugging, got frustrated, went back to manual triggers.

❌ **One prompt for all meetings**: Different meetings need different approaches. My exec update template failed miserably for technical deep-dives.

❌ **Ignoring edge cases**: Urgent meetings and sensitive topics need human judgment. I learned to recognize when NOT to use the agent.

## Challenges Encountered

### Challenge 1: Information Overload
**Problem:** Fed the AI too much context (last 5 meeting notes, entire project spec, all open tickets). Result: Confused, generic output.

**Solution:** Be selective. I now provide:
- Last meeting summary only (not full notes)
- 3-5 key context points (not entire docs)
- Specific questions to focus the AI

### Challenge 2: Accuracy Validation
**Problem:** How do I know the AI isn't hallucinating action items or missing critical decisions?

**Solution:** 
- Always review AI output before sharing
- Use AI to draft, human to validate
- Cross-check action items against raw notes
- Build trust gradually with low-stakes meetings first

### Challenge 3: Team Adoption
**Problem:** How do I share meeting summaries generated by AI without stakeholders questioning quality?

**Solution:**
- Don't mention AI unless asked
- Focus on value (faster, more consistent)
- Improve quality through iteration
- Build trust before revealing the process

## Practical Applications for TPMs

If you're just starting, here's what I'd recommend:

### Start Small
Pick ONE meeting type that:
- Happens regularly (weekly or more)
- Has clear structure
- Isn't highly sensitive
- Would benefit from better documentation

### Build Your First Template
1. Write down what you'd want in a perfect meeting brief
2. Create a prompt that asks for exactly that
3. Include specific context (meeting title, attendees, program state)
4. Test it on 3 meetings
5. Refine based on what's missing or wrong

### Iterate Based on Value
Ask yourself:
- Did this save me time?
- Is the output high enough quality to share?
- What would make it 10% better?

Don't aim for perfection. Aim for "better than manual."
